Cover-Up, Skirrid, and Sudden Death!

Date: 2028-10-27 14:13 | Tags: old games, tile games

Cover-Up is a 1971 uncredited game, published by Spear's Games.

The game is played in the following 16x16 board:


Each player has a set of 24 polyominoes: eight unit squares, eight 1x2 tiles, eight 2x2 tiles. Two of the 1x2 and two of the 2x2 have a doubling marker (for scoring purposes).

The game is to be played by two, three or four players. For two players, the rules say to play only in half of the board.

Rules:

  • The first player, in his first move, plays a friendly piece at the center of the board
  • One his turn, the player drops a friendly piece on empty squares
    • the black squares are obstacles and cannot be used
    • the dropped piece must be adjacent to another piece (of either color) already on board (diagonal corners are not adjacent)
  • Each dropped piece scores for each one of its covered squares:
    • unmarked squares value 1 point
    • a square marked by a number values that number
    • any tile placed with a doubling marker, doubles its total value
  • If one player does not have valid moves, the adversary moves as many turns as he can (thus ending the game)
  • Wins the player with the highest score

This game predates Blokus for almost 30 years. The main difference is the scoring, that is replaced in Blokus by a stalemate goal. Despite this minor distinction, Blokus is a game immensely more popular. An example that a bit of viral luck mixed with timing, aesthetics, rule simplification, perhaps marketing, does make a world of difference! Also, the world after Tetris became somewhat different ;-)
 
A pertinent comment regarding the scoring system by Adam A. at BGG
 
What is most interesting about Cover Up is that it has a very Blokus-type feel to it, much earlier before Blokus ever came out. Funny enough, they took a pure idea of area enclosure and tile placement and muddied it up with a point system that is super clumsy and at worst unfair. A game that starts off interesting, trying to set yourself up for points while avoiding the same goal for your opponent. However, by the end it just becomes a game of chicken, delaying the need to place a piece beside the crazy bonus point corners that will win you the game hands down. Funny that the Spear's edition has much lower bonus point values, which makes sense, as they actually break the game in a way. Cover Up is a good idea that sadly went nowhere and was probably bettered upon independently 25 years later by Blokus.

Another precursor of Blokus was 1977's Skirrid by Mark Eliot and Brian Taylor.

The pieces for one player (there is another set with a different color):


and the board: 

The first tile is placed at the board center (as shown above). Tile placements are adjacent to previous tiles, as in Cover-Up. A placement score is the sum of numbers covered by a piece, with a possible multiplier for certain tiles. There is an extra rule for blocking the placement of enemy tiles. Another similar game is 1978's Sudden Death!. The BGG description,
 
Sudden Death is a surround placement type of game. During your turn you place down 1 of 3 different plastic pieces made up of either two, three or four squares. The board consists of 441 squares and each player starts on his own side. The object is to block the other players to prevent them from playing any more pieces. You are allowed to jump over one square.
