Qui Vive

Date: 2027-01-12 17:19 | Tags: old games

Qui Vive is a 1984 game by Hal Renko and Sam Edwards, and was a video-game for the Commodore 64.


play the game at a C64 emulator

The program was published at Papersoft magazine, issue #44 (year 2):


link to magazine 

This game, fascinating and very ancient, is played on a 5x5 board.  Each player has five pieces and must try to arrange them so as to obtain a symmetrical configuration. In the version we present here, your opponent is the VIC d3. At the beginning of the game, the board is empty and, alternately, you and the computer must place your pieces on it by entering the desired coordinates (A1, B3, D2, etc.). Once all the pieces are placed, you can start moving them [pieces can move to any empty square!], trying to achieve a winning configuration. To do this, you must first type the coordinates of the piece you want to move, then the coordinates of the desired destination position.

This text do not mention what are the winning configurations. But fortunately, archive.org has them. There are seven:

. o o o o o . x .
. . . . . . . x .
. x . . . . . x .
x x x . . . . x .
. x . . . . . x .
. . . o . o . . .
. . . . o . . . x
. . . o . o . x .
. . . . . . x . .
. . x . . x . . .
. x . . x . . . .
x . . o . . . o .
. x . . o . o . .
. . x . . o . . .

that is, a five in-a-row (either vertical, horizontal or diagonal), a cross (orthogonal or diagonal), or a 'wing' (a vertical or horizontal V).

A note about the C64 program:

The program is one of the most interesting in this book.  It contains a list of not only all 42 possible winning patterns but also over 100 particularly strong situations from which a player has a chance of making either one of two winning patterns.  During the game the computer monitors this list in the light of the situation on the board and assesses what its best move is.
