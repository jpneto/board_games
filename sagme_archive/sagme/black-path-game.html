Black Path Game

Date: 2029-10-26 12:12 | Tags: old games, pencil & paper, tile games

Black Path Game is a 1960 game by Larry Black, published at A.K. Peters.

The game is played on a square grid of size N (say 8x8). The game is a pencil & paper game.

If played on a physical board, the players must have access to enough tiles of three types, each representing one way to connect two black lines between two edges (eg, use a Trax set).

Rules:

  • Initially a cross tile is placed on the top-let corner of the grid
  • On his turn, the player draws a new tile, adjacent to another already on board, extending the lines already drawn
  • The player that draws a line connecting one of the grid's edges, loses the game.

Michele Spino refers to a 1992 variant proposed by Jan Kristian Haugland called Blackout:

This is a variant of the Black Path Game that eliminates the simple winning strategy. When a player steers the path into another piece, he gets another move. I.e., he keeps playing until the last piece steers the path directly into an empty square (or off the board). Recommended board sizes are 6x6 and 8x8.

These tiles are used, more famously, in Trax. The curved ones are called Truchet curves and are the subject of a 2008 article by Cameron Browne named Truchet Curves and Surfaces.
