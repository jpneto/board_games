The Game of Football and Tobac

Date: 2027-08-20 07:46 | Tags: older games, checkers

The Game of Football is a 1892 uncredited game, published at Parker Brothers.

The game is played on a 7x7 board, and uses a Checkers short-jump, but in orthogonal directions (a bit like Dama, the Turkish Draughts). When a stone jumps over the ball, that stone carries the ball until it is jumped over or crosses the goal line.

Tobac is a 1943 uncredited game, published by Milton Bradley.

The game is played in the following board (players start at the right and left edges),

Each player has four pieces that start on the first row. There is also a marble that can be placed on top of each piece

Rules:

  • Initially, each player places the marble on one of her pieces
  • On her turn, the player slides a friendly piece over a line of diagonally adjacent empty circles
    • the piece with the marble cannot move
  • The marble can be transferred to an adjacent piece, and so on, multiple times in a single turn
  • Wins the player that brings her marble to the last row

The original rules do not state if passing the marble takes an entire turn, or if it can be done just after the piece move. My suggestion is the latter option, to keep the game more dynamic, and closer to the sport theme.

There are several modern games with a similar approach. Here are some:

1983's Catch'it by Max Gerchambeau.

BGG description:

Each player has seven pawns with hole on top and a ball. One player put the ball. In his turn, Player may move two of his pawns. If he has the ball, he must place the ball to hole of another pawn at the end of his move. Pawns may move one of two ways: adjacent square or jump over other pawn vertically, horizontally or diagonally. The goal is to place the ball past the opposing line.

2005's Knight Moves (aka, Razzle Dazzle).


BGG description:

Two combatants must outsmart each other in their attempt to maneuver their sphere to the opposite side of the playing field. But to do so, each rival must first lay down a course of wooden blocks from which their sphere can be catapulted. Moving the blocks like Knights in the game of Chess, precise positioning is of the utmost importance in order for the sphere to capture the win.

In this fast-paced game, suggestive of basketball meets Chess, two players try to move their marble to the opposite side of the board, using 5 wooden "blocks" as passing instruments. A player may either pass the ball or move a block like a Knight in a game of Chess. The ball may be passed in any direction, but the block with a ball may not be moved. It is competitive, it is strategic, and it is quick.
