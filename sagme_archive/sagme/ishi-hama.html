Ishi-Hama

Date: 2027-09-17 17:16 | Tags: rotating pieces, new games

Ishi-Hama is a 2004 game by Christophe Berg, published at Des Jeux sur un Plateau magazine.

The game is played on a 9x9 board, initially empty.

Each player has a set of the following 14 tiles:

  • five tiles with 4 branches
  • five tiles with 3 branches
  • two tiles with 2 branches in line
  • two tiles with 2 branches in a 90º angle

Rules:

  • Initially, each player drops a friendly tile in an empty square not orthogonally adjacent to any other tile
  • Afterwards, on his turn, the player either:
    • drops a tile from his reserve on an empty square
    • picks a tile which then can be moved one or two squares orthogonally and/or rotated
    • swaps two tiles already on board
  • Any sequence of five connected tiles scores one point; the player then removes these five tiles and gives four to the adversary that adds them to his reserve
    • the remaining tile can be used to mark the point and is not used again
  • When a player picks or drops one tile, he must continue to use that color until he scores a point
  • Wins the player that scores seven points
