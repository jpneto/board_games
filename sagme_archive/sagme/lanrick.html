Lanrick

Date: 2028-12-29 09:21 | Tags: older games

Lanrick is a 1878 game by Lewis Carroll (yes, that Lewis Carroll).

The game is played on a 8x8 board,


Each player has five pieces. There's also a neutral piece (the mark).

Definition:

  • The border is the 28 outside squares near the edges
  • The field are the remaining  36 squares
  • The rendezvous is the 3x3 area surrounding the mark

Rules:

  • Initially the first player drops all pieces of both colors inside the border; then the second player places the mark inside the field in a way that the rendezvous does not intersect the border
  • On his turn, the player either:
    • moves a friendly piece to an empty square or on top of the marker
      • pieces move like a Chess queen, sliding over a straight line of empty squares
      • the total amount of steps a player can move in a turn are given by the total number of pieces a player has on board (except at the first turn, where the first player can only move twice)
      • these movements can used by one or more pieces
    • moves the mark to an adjacent empty square
      • when the mark is moved, all pieces inside the rendezvous are moved together with it
      • the rendezvous must not intersect the border
      • the rendezvous must not include new pieces from the moving player
      • the rendezvous must not, with its movement, move a piece to an occupied square
  •  Wins the player that moves all his pieces to the rendezvous [check below]

The rules state: this privilege, of moving the rendezvous-mark, is not allowed to the Player who laid it down. I'm not sure if this means that only the first player is able to move the mark. If that is the case, that would make the game very unbalanced.
