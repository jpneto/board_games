King's Court

Date: 2030-02-15 09:52 | Tags: checkers, old games

King's Court, aka, Supercheckers, is a 1986 game by Christopher Wroth, published at MW Games.

The game is played in the following board:


initial setup

Each player has 24 pieces. The central 4x4 area is the King's Court.

Rules

  • Initially, each player moves a friendly piece into the court; the second player must enter the court from the opposite side of the board
  • Afterwards, on his turn, the player selects a friendly piece and either:
    • moves it to an orthogonal adjacent empty square
    • jumps it over a piece of either color, landing on the immediate next square that must be empty
      • jumping over enemy pieces will capture them
      • jumps can be multiple
  • Pieces only move and jump over the light squares (as in Checkers).
  • Pieces can move in and out of the court
  • The player that, at any time, does not have at least one piece inside the court, loses the game

This is clearly a Checkers variant. The game does not have promotions, replacing them with a goal of always occupying the central area. The game also has elements of Konane, the traditional Hawaiian game, given the checkered setup that releases two areas for fighting when the two pieces initially enter the court.

After the first phase of mutual capturing, the game might transition to a quieter tactical phase. However, contrary to Konane, which has a stalemate goal (first unable to move, loses the game), here it is not obvious that the game can enter a cyclic repetition of moves, if neither player wishes to risk their position. The game should have considered this potential problem in the ruleset.

There is a ZRF to play the game.
