Push

Date: 2027-08-27 07:00 | Tags: old games, new games

Push is a 1977 game by Gilbert Obermair, published at Spear's Games.

The game is played in a 5x5 grid designed for marbles to be pushed over the board. Each player has 13 pieces. Initially, 25 pieces are placed in a chess-like pattern, leaving one piece off board.

Rules:

  • The player with the off-board piece uses it at one of the entrances labeled 0-9, pushing out a piece of either color
  • Wins the player that makes a 3x3 square of his pieces

Notice that a player might push a friendly piece in order to move again. In fact, this type of tactic is needed to build such a demanding shape of nine pieces.

The original rules:

Push is a percursor of a more well-known game, 1987's Abalone.

§

Another game using this pushing mechanism is Reiner Knizia's Limelight, published in Games & Puzzles Magazine #14 (May 1995).

From the BGG description:

Limelight is played on a 4x4 grid with the corners cut off using pieces in two colors. White plays first and has 11 pieces, black has 10. On a turn a player enters a piece on one of the edge spaces, pushing a piece there to the next space and so on. However, pieces may not be pushed off the board. When all pieces have been played the board will be full. The player with the most pieces in the nine central squares wins. The game can be played on a larger board with more pieces. If the center region has an even number of spaces and the players tie, the first player wins.
