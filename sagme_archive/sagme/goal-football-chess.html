GOAL Fussball-Schach (Football Chess) is a 1958 uncredited game, published at Tip Verlag.

The game is played in the following board:


initial setup

Each player has one goalkeeper, two defenders and eight regular pieces. There's a share piece, the ball.

The game simulates a match of football but the game is not a Chess variant, all pieces are similar (no royal pieces), and there are no captures.

Football Chess is an exciting new form of entertainment for everyone who enjoys a strategy game.  

Football Chess is much easier to learn than chess but still offers unlimited combinations and goal-scoring opportunities.  

Eleven players stand opposite each other with the ball. Shortly after the kickoff, within just a few moves, both sides engage in a lively battle. Goals, outs, corners, offsides, free kicks, back passes, assists, covering, etc., work just as they do in real football.  

This is the first game built entirely around football rules, and it is undoubtedly the first game in a long time to excite all audiences.  

The thrilling moments in Football Chess cannot be described—they must be experienced!  

No one should be discouraged by the seemingly long game rules from playingFootball Chess. The following rules have been deliberately written in detail and are therefore easy to understand. It is pleasant when the rules explain everything.  

Game Rules  

1. Partners and Game Duration

Football Chess is played by two players or teams.  A game (one match) generally lasts 2 × 15 minutes, but the teams may agree on any game duration they prefer.  The winner is the team that scores the most goals. If both opponents score the same number of goals or no goals at all, the game is considered a draw.  

2. Playing Pieces  

The following types of pieces are distinguished by their shape:  

  • 1 Goalkeeper (Goalie)  
  • 2 Defenders (Backs)  
  • 3 Midfielders (Halfs)  
  • 5 Forwards (Strikers)  

Additionally,1 ball is used. 

(Note: There is no difference in the shape of the playing pieces.)*

3. Placement of the Pieces  

The starting setup is as follows:  

-Goalkeeper in the middle of the 5-goal area;  
-Defenders on the two points in the penalty area;  
-Midfielders and Forwards on the remaining points;  
-Ball in the center of the field.  

At halftime (e.g., after 15 minutes of play) or after a goal is scored, the pieces are placed back in their starting positions.  

4. Movement of the Pieces  

With the exception of the goalkeeper (see §11), all pieces can move in any direction—forward, backward, sideways, and diagonally—but always in a straight line.  

A piece can move one or more spaces butcan never jump over another piece, nor can it be placed on a space already occupied by another piece.  

Only the ball can be jumped over under certain conditions (see §6).  

5. Movement of the Ball  

The ballcan never move alone; it must always be moved in combination with a piece. This means that the piece pushes or kicks the ball forward.  

A piece can only play the ball if it lies one or more spaces ahead of it in adirect line without obstruction.

At the end of the move,there must be at least one empty space between the ball and the piece. This means that the ball can also be moved two spaces ahead of the piece.  

If at the start of a move the ball is directly in front of a piece and is only moved one space forward, the piece remains in its current position. 

6. Jumping Over the Ball  

If a piece is positioned next to the ball on the field and the team wants to move the ball in the opposite direction, the piece is allowed tojump over the ballonly if the space behind the ball is free.  

Jumping over the balldoes not count as a separate move. The piece may also jump over the ball without playing it. In this case, it is considered a simple movewithout the ball.  

7. Turns and Moves  

Both teams take turns playing. There are two different types of moves:  

1.Simple Move: This consists of moving a piecewithout the ball. This is typically done to prepare a combination (positioning for a pass) or toblock an opponent's piece or shooting path.  
   - During a simple move, the piece may move directly (without any gaps) to the ball's position.  

2. Double Move: This happens when a piecepasses the ball to another piece of the same team in such a way that the second piece can immediately continue the movement.  
   - In adouble move, it is not allowed tofirst move with the ball and then movewithout it. The order must be reversed.  
   - However, a player is allowed tofirst make a move without the ball and then, in the second part of the turn, move with the ball, provided the game situation allows it.  
   - The same piece may not perform both parts of a double move.
   
8. Game Start (Anstoß).

The lot decides which player starts the game. The starting move is made from the center square (i. 11 for white, i. 13 for black). This square is initially considered the center line or the right or left half of the field (also field H. 12 or K. 12) and is played from there, observing the basic rules, with the other player moving another piece of the same color, to make a double move or to refrain from doing so. Each player is free to make this first double move or to decline it.

9. The Field Play.

Each player tries to bring the ball into a position through combinations so that they can push it into the opponent's undefended goal field. In the process, the player always tries to avoid good defensive positions in front of their own goal, and therefore pays attention to protecting their own goal from the opponent's attack.

10. The Penalty Area.

The penalty area, also called the "16-meter area" in football, encompasses the 28 squares in front of the goal and is defined by a line. Within it, no more than 5 figures of the defending party (excluding the goalkeeper) may operate in the penalty area. On the other hand, the attacking player can move any number of figures in the penalty area.

11. The Goalkeeper (Goalie).

The goalkeeper can move to any of the 5 squares of the goal line, but must not leave them.  However, he has the right to catch any figure that moves towards the goal.

The goalkeeper may only play the ball if it is within one of these penalty area squares, and then only in a direct line (straight or diagonal) away.

The goalkeeper may only play the ball up to the center line of the playing field (line 12).  In contrast to the regular field players, the goalkeeper does not follow the movement of the ball, but remains stationary in the goal field from where he played the ball.

12. The Defenders.

The defenders can move like the runners or attackers, freely on the entire playing field, with the only restriction that one of the two defenders must always remain within their own penalty area. A defender can move from his own penalty area into the opponent's penalty area; both defenders must always be within their own 16-meter zone.

13. Blocking the Ball.

On the 8 squares directly adjacent to the ball, no more than 6 figures, a maximum of 3 from each player, may be placed.  At least two squares must always remain open for the ball to be played. The ball is free if it can be played from the ball.

14. Out.

The ball is "Out" if it lands on the squares of lines A and R, which are the side boundaries of the playing field. The ball may never be played onto one of these squares. However, figures can use the out-of-bounds squares like any other square.

15. Behind and Corner.

The squares behind the boundary lines to the right and left next to the goal are "Behind." If one playing party moves their own figure next to the ball into their own "Behind" goal, the opposing party has the right to a corner (Eckball).

To do this, this party places the ball on the line of the same side as the outermost field within the out-of-bounds line (i.e., after the fields B. 2, B. 22, Q. 2, or Q. 22). Then they place their figure closest to the ball on the neighboring out-of-bounds field of line 2 or 22.

This figure then plays the ball onto line 2 or 22 in such a way that another figure of the playing party can score a goal with the second move of this double move.

16. Offside.

Offside or Abseits is only possible within the penalty area. Offside occurs when a figure of the attacking party reaches the same height as the rearmost figure of the defending party (goalkeeper excluded).

The defending party can prevent an offside position by moving one of their own figures past an attacker.

A goal can be scored from an offside position, but the relevant party must move their offside figure back as soon as they are in turn.
