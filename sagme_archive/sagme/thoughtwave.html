Thoughtwave

Date: 2028-06-23 15:16 | Tags: old games, connection

Thoughtwave, aka Ultra, is a 1974 game by Eric Solomon, published at Parker Spiele.

The game is played on a 10x10 board,

Each player has the same set of 24 tiles,

A different aesthetics for the pieces (from the Jeux Strategie website):

Definition:

  • A path is a sequence of matching tiles, of either color, that connects two separate board squares

Rules:

  • One player owns the South/North sides, the other player owns the East/West sides
  • On his turn, the player drops one friendly piece on an empty square
    • the tile placement is only valid if its entries match all entries of the adjacent tiles (of either color)
  • Wins the player that makes a path connecting his two opposite sides

Cameron Browne, on his 2005's Connection Games book (pages 207-212), says that the board is too small to block effectively an attack. Any small advantage is very difficult to counter. One balancing feature is the reduce number of options a player has (not many pieces per tile type) that might limit how the attack unrolls. 

Quinn Swanger suggests that a larger board allows for a better game. To play in a 14x14 board, the set of tiles should be four crosses, twelve tees, ten straights, twenty bends and two terminal tiles.
