Posit

Date: 2030-03-29 20:49 | Tags: modern games, new games, stack pieces

Posit is a 1991 game by Shinichi Tobita, published at Yonezawa corp.

The game is played on a 6x6 board.

initial setup

Each player has a single piece (starting one at d1, the other at c6). There are also 104 shared cubes. These cubes will be placed on the board, making stacks of different heights,

in the middle of a Posit match

Rules

  • On his turn, the player first moves his piece, then places a cube on the board
    • Pieces can move to an orthogonally or diagonally adjacent square, either on their own level or, at most, one level up
      • Pieces cannot (a) move on top of stacks of three cubes, (b) move down a level, or (c) move up two levels in the same turn
    • Cubes can be placed on empty squares or be placed on top of other cubes, up to a maximum stack of three cubes
  • A player without valid moves, loses the game.

The game was ported to a iPhone app, being renamed CubeSieger.

§

Posit is quite obscure, which is unfortunate. The game has simple rules, but intricate strategy and gameplay. The potential of the game can be seen on its reiterations.

One is 1998 Quivive from Gigamic. Here, the 49 disks start already at the board. On each turn, the player moves his piece (diagonally or orthogonally) to an unoccupied stack and then removes an unoccupied disk. There is no height limit for the piece to move into, but the piece cannot move to an empty square (ie, which already has no disks). Quivive was later rebranded as Sputnik, which added a quite silly Space theme.

Quivive seems a less interesting game, since it does not consider the stack's height difference. But it had much more commercial success...

Other similar games, also related with Amazons, are Climbers and 3D Amazons.

The game has two modes to win: (a) either a worker moves to a level-3 tower, or (b) the adversary in his turn, cannot complete his turn.

This is a good example of how timing, marketing, and good artistic work can make a world of difference in delivering what is, in its essence, just a good abstract game.
