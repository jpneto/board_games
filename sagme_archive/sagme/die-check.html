Die-Check

Date: 2027-10-01 19:57 | Tags: chess, old games, dice

Die-Check is a 1972 game by Benedict Einstein and Benoit Launier, published by BFM (among others).

The game is played on a 9x9 board,


the initial setup

Each player has six regular dice (the strategic cubes), two double pieces (the shields) and one royal piece (the master).

Rules:

  • On his turn, the player moves one friendly piece.
    • A strategic cube moves the number of squares given by its current top face
      • the moving direction can be forward, backward or sideways
      • each move folds the cube to its next face (there are no jumps)
      • the move is on a straight line, but the player may change direction (left or right) once
      • a strategic cube captures by replacement (except the shield, see below)

moving a strategic cube by successive foldings

    • The master can move to an orthogonal square and captures by replacement (except the shield, see below)
    • A shield (which occupies two squares) can move one of its halves to an orthogonally adjacent empty square of the other half (this implies that a shield always rotate, it never slides)
      • A shield cannot capture, it is a purely defensive piece
      • To capture a shield, both halves must be attacked, and one of its  halves must be captured by a strategic cube

the shield has both halves under attack,
and will be captured after dice 2 captures its right half

  • Wins the player that checkmates the adversary's master piece

The original rules are collected by François Haffner at l’Escale à jeux website (including extra rules/variants).

The game was remarketed as Cubulus in 1999 (not to be confused with Gigamic's homonymous game).
