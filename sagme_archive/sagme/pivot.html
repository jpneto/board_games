Pivot

Date: 2028-01-28 15:24 | Tags: flip pieces, old games

Pivot, aka Imperator, is a 1975 game by J. Brian McCarthy, published at Clipper.

The game is played on the following board:

There are twenty triangular pieces of double color faces. Each player receives ten.

Rules:

  • Initially, on her turn, the player drops a piece, with her color on top, into an empty position
  • Afterwards, the players, on their turn, move one friendly piece
    • a piece can move to any triangle that shares an edge or a corner (which means a maximum of twelve adjacencies!)
    • however, pieces cannot jump over other pieces (this tends to restrict the pieces' move range)
  • When an piece, or group of connected pieces, is surrounded (similar to Go) it flips color
    • At the board's edge, those positions near holes protect red pieces which cannot be captured
    • At the board's edge, those positions not near holes protect white pieces which cannot be captured
  • Wins the player that stalemates the adversary, or reduces her army to two pieces

The game seems to have serious flaws, namely there are not enough pieces to surround the other player. Perhaps this problem could be mitigated if the surrounded pieces were to be placed in the player's reserve, to be played again somewhere else. In this case, a small advantage could be multiplied by this Shogi-like mechanism. This is not totally satisfactory, since we would be paying more decidability with less drama...

The author also has two other games, that I know of, with serious rule design problems: Contra (already posted here at Sagme's) and Track. C'est dommage, the game designs are pretty and deserved better rulesets.
