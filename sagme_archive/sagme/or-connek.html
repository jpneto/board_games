Or-Connek

Date: 2029-12-28 15:26 | Tags: go, new games

Or-Connek is a pre-2008 game by Colin Leamon, published (?) at Shinermons Games.

The game is played on a 15x15 board:

Each player has enough pieces of his color (called ai).

The next rules are based on a review which does not explain all the details. It is mentioned territories in the context of Go, so I assume they have the same meaning here.

Rules

  • Initially, players drop friendly pieces over the nine 'star points' points of the board (center, 4-4 points and 4-8 points)
  • Afterwards, a player drops one piece on an empty square
    • if that square has N>1 adjacent pieces (orthogonal or diagonal), the player extends the placed piece, with exactly N-1 pieces in an orthogonal or diagonal line
      • it is illegal to choose a line of squares including friendly pieces, or going off-board
      • any enemy pieces on the line, are captured by replacement
  • Once a territory is closed, the pieces making the territory can no longer be captured, nor the territory invaded
  • Wins the player with the highest sum of captured pieces plus controlled territory

The review mentions the closed-territory rule:

What undermines play in my experience is that a player cannot place an ai in any captured area and that pieces in the "wall" surrounding a captured area are themselves immune from capture. Further, no limit on the size of captured area exists in either theory or in practice. A player can form a line right across the board and so capture half the board. Enemy pieces within captured areas are also captured and removed from the board. [...] I think the very nature of the criticisms I have cited suggest the correction. Namely, if ai in surrounded territory are not captured, if no pieces are immune from capture and if ai can be placed in any empty square subject to the restriction intrinsic to the manner of placement, this becomes a simply great game.

I share the reviewer's opinion.
