Fibonacci

Date: 2030-05-24 16:57 | Tags: chess, new games

Fibonacci is a 1990 game by Thomas Naylor, published at Thomas Naylor and Associates.

The game is played on a hexhex of size 6:

expert setup
diagrams from Stephen Tavener's AiAi software

Each player has six support pieces, six strike pieces (with the arrows) and one centrepiece (the royal piece). 

The game uses the notion of group, ie, a set of connected pieces of the same color and the same type.

Rules:

  • The move sequence is: one move for Player1, then two moves for Player2, then three moves... until all players have six moves
    • However, each enemy strike piece adjacent to the centrepiece, will decrease the number of moves by one
  • On her turn, the player moves a friendly support or strike piece to a hex adjacent to the group the moving piece belongs
    • if the moving hex is occupied (any piece of either color), then the two pieces swap positions
  • Wins the player that surrounds the enemy centrepiece with friendly strike pieces

This is a strange game, not in a bad sense. The six moves per turn, and the average number of options for each move, makes for a enormous branching factor. 

Having these many options could produce a decisiveness problem, ie, being very hard to finish a match. But, near the endgame, players will get less and less free moves -- given their centrepieces will start to get surrounded -- which contributes to some positional stability.

It can be played on AiAi.
