Thataway

Date: 2027-12-24 08:28 | Tags: rotating pieces, chess, new games

Thataway is a 2000 game by William Daniel 'Dan' Troyka, published online.

The game is played in a 7x7 board with the following setup:


 Each player starts with:

  • three Pointers (b3, d3, f3)
  • two Crosses (a2, g2)
  • two Stars (b1, f1)
  • three Pods (d1, c2, e2)

Rules:

  • The piece movements are as follow:
    • Pointers slide over the line defined by their markings, they can be rotated 45º or 90º
    • Crosses slide over the two lines defined by their markings, they can be rotated 45º (so they can act either as a Chess Rook or as a Bishop)
    • Stars act like Chess Queens
    • Pods move one orthogonal space, and can be replaced for a previously captured Star
  • On her turn, the player moves, rotates or replaces one of his pieces
  • Captures are by replacement
  • The player without Pods loses the game

An example:


Black moves his Cross b6-a6, opening a trap for White.
If White captures the b5 Pointer, Black replies a6-a1
which dooms the a2 Star. 

The game is a Chess variant, since pods are royal pieces. However, Pods are quite different than Chess Kings. The first difference is the number, of course, there are three of them. And this multiplicity gives the player a question to ponder: should I sacrifice one or two Pods to make for the much needed fire power that Stars provide? But any Pod a player loses, makes it easier for the adversary to win. All in all, a very fine game.
