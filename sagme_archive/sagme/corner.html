Corner

Date: 2026-12-29 10:45 | Tags: old games, moku

Corner, aka Intersection, is a 1974 game by Sid Sackson, published at Ravensburger.

The game is played in a 6x6 board, with two markers for rows and columns:

There is also 36 pieces, namely eight yellow, seven red, six green, five blue, four white, and three grey and black pieces. 

Initially these pieces are placed at random on the board (or by any other procedure agreed by the players)

Rules:

  • One player selects a row, the other selects a column (using the markers)
  • Each player takes turns moving their marker along their respective edge and takes the piece located at the intersection of the indicated row and column.
    • The players cannot move their markers to a coordinate that has an empty intersection or a black piece (black pieces cannot be captured)
    • The game ends where there is no valid moves
  • For each color, players score points based on the number of pieces they have collected:
    • one piece = 1 point
    • two pieces = 3 points
    • three pieces = 6 points
    • four pieces = 10 points
    • five pieces = 13 points
    • six pieces = 16 points
    • seven pieces = 20 points
    • eight pieces = 24 points
    • These points are doubled for colors in which the opponent has no pieces
  • The game must be played twice, so that each player can start first.
  • Wins the player with highest score

A game with a similar mechanism is 1976's Crossover by Trevor Truran. 

The BGG description:

Crossover is played on a 5x5 grid with 25 pieces with different colors on each side. Two markers are also required which move along two edges of the board - one is controlled by each player. On a turn a player moves their marker and then places a piece at the space the two markers are pointing at, if it is empty. If a player cannot move to point to an empty space they point to an occupied space and flip the piece there over, regardless of which side is currently showing. The winner is the first to get four pieces of their color in a continuous orthogonal line.
