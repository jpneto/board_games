Yonmoque

Date: 2028-12-08 15:04 | Tags: flip pieces, new games, moku

Yonmoque is a 1997 game by Mitsuo Yamamoto, published at Logy Games.

The game is played on a 5x5 board, initially empty:


the squares with blue/white markings are neutral squares
(neither blue nor white)

The game comes with twelve pieces with sides of distinct colors (like in Othello). Each player takes six pieces.

Rules:

  • Blue starts [color here is important, since there's a color asymmetry in the board]
  • On his turn, the player either:
    • drops a friendly piece in an empty square
    • moves a friendly piece already on board to an empty square
      • a piece moves like the Chess king
      • if the piece is in a square of its color, it can also move like a Chess bishop over a diagonal sequence of squares of that color (so, if a Blue piece reaches a neutral square, it stops there)
      • a moving piece can flip a pair of enemy pieces via custodian capture
  • Wins the player that makes a 4 in-a-row via a piece movement, or if the opponent's makes a 5 in-a-row

Note that dropping a piece does not produce captures/flips, neither makes a winning 4 in-a-row (the game just continues).

Blue movements are seriously less mobile than White's. I assume that is made to compensate the first-player advantage typical of moku games. But I wonder if the handicap is not too strong.
