G.H.Q., aka Battle, is a 1940 uncredited game, published at  E. E. Fairchild Corporation.

The next text is the official rules for the game:

OBJECT OF THE GAME – To take the sector of enemy territory by capturing your opponent’s men, artillery and planes with the least possible loss of your own playing pieces.

PLAYING EQUIPMENT – A playing board on which all action takes place. Each player has the following personnel and equipment:

  • Two Airplanes (Large game pieces with hub & top)
  • Two Pieces of Artillery (Large bullet-shaped game pieces)
  • Eleven Men
  • One Pontoon Bridge

TO PLAY – Each player chooses his colored playing pieces. He places all of his playing pieces in the General Headquarters corresponding to his color. There are two parts to the play action of this game.

1st – Place pieces on the board
2nd – Move pieces on the board

  • Each player (or side) has one play in turn, a play consisting of either placing or moving a piece. Airplanes, artillery and men may be placed and moved freely in any direction except diagonally.
  • Before a player places or moves men or artillery across the river, he must place a bridge and cannot use his opponent’s bridge.
  • A man or bridge may be placed in enemy territory only two spaces after a bridge is placed.
  • A bridge, once used, cannot be moved. No gun, plane or man may be placed on a bridge, the move has to be from one side of the river to the other.
  • The first player to move may place anyone of his playing pieces – man, artillery, or plane – behind the front on the board. Once successive plays begin, a player may place new pieces more or more men already on the board.
  • Remember – After the first man piece (not artillery or airplane) is on the board, following new pieces must be placed not more than 3 spaces from the previously placed men. If may be next to either a friendly or an opponent piece, but must not be a space between the two.

TO MAKE A CAPTURE

By Surrounding With Men – Capture an opponent’s pieces of any kind between two friendly men pieces so long as the two men are not more than 3 spaces apart. This may be done in a straight line – vertically, horizontally, or diagonally. He is out of the game until replaced.

By Artillery After Direct Hit – After a plane or piece of artillery has been placed on the board, they may move only in a straight line – never diagonally – for any number of spaces (or until stopped by a friendly piece, a direct hit may be made by artillery and destroyed 3 spaces from it.

When an enemy piece is hit by artillery, the piece is removed from the board.

GENERAL RULES

  • No two men or other individual pieces may be placed on the same space. 
  • The game is over when one player places three pieces (men, plane, or artillery or between them) in the opponent’s headquarters.
  • No two like pieces jump over another piece. No bridge may be captured. No capture may be made with airplane.

TO WIN – When no further move may be made by a player, the player able to make the last move wins.
