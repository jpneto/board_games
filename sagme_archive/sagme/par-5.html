Par: 5

Date: 2028-08-11 07:46 | Tags: boardless, new games, tile games, moku

Par: 5 is a 2002 game by Serge Bouquet, published by Alveole.


the pieces on the tiles are just score markers

There is no board. There are 60 double hex tiles: nine double-green, nine double-yellow, and 42 bicolored.

Each player starts with his nine unicolored tiles, and 21 bicolored tiles (see note below).

Rules:

  • On his turn, the player drops a friendly tile that must be adjacent to two hexes already on board 
    • the only exception is the first tile, of course
  • When all tiles are placed, players count the number of 5 in-a-rows they have, each one worth 1 point
    • if two mokus are aligned in the same direction, the last hexagon of the first counts as the first hexagon of the second
      • so, a 9 in-a-row scores 2 points; a 13 in-a-row scores 3 points, etc.
  • Wins the player with the highest score
    • in case of a draw, wins the player that made the first 5 in-a-row

Scoring multiple mokus in sequence is so much harder that the scoring should have increased more than just proportionally. One possible new scoring could add 1 point for each extra hex so, say, a 7 in-a-row would value 3 points.

note: in the original rules players have five tiles on-hand, and a dominoe-like way to get a new piece inside a sack, which would imply hidden information. Also, the rules stated that unicolored tiles only are to be used by the respective player. This new proposal makes this variant an abstract game, without changing much of its dynamics.
