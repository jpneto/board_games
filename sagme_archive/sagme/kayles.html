Kayles

Date: 2027-05-03 17:29 | Tags: older games, pencil & paper

Kayles is a 1908 game by Henry Dudeney.

Kayles comes from the French word quilles, meaning pins from Bowling.

The basic idea is to start with a sequence of stones/pins. Each player, in their turn, must remove one or two adjacent stones. The first player unable to move, loses the game.

This is a basic game with a trivial winning strategy, remove one or two stones from the middle in order to create two equal sequences of stones (say, if you start with 19 stones, remove the middle one to create two sequences of 9 stones). Then just mirror the adversary move.

The game was studied in Combinatorial Graph Theory, which found that Kayles can be expressed as NIM, one of the seminal CGT games. So, each Kayles position has a NIM-value (also called a nimber). Check the wikipedia page for further details.

A more interesting version is its misère variant, where the first player unable to move, wins the game.

I found in Bu Xiang, Encyclopedia of Board Games, 棋弈游戏大全 (1989) another variant of Kayles (I don't know the year of this game) called The Pairs Game, 对子棋,

 

In this game each player must remove exactly two adjacent stones, and the player unable to move loses. This prevents the trivial winning strategy of Kayles.

The values of this game, however, are already computed. They are listed in A215721, values of N for which the 1 X N domino-covering game is a second player win. And the game can also be solved using the Sprague-Grundy theory that established that all impartial games (like these two) are equivalent to a one-heap game of NIM.
