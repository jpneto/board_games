Toi-Toi, die Grabenhopser

Date: 2030-08-23 11:56 | Tags: older games

Toi-Toi, die Grabenhopser is a 1950s uncredited game, published at Die Toi Gesellschaftsspiele.

The game is played in the following board:

Each player has a home base in their 'Toi' 3x3 area.

Players have nine pieces each, three of them marked 'T', 'O', and 'I' (the TOI pieces). The pieces start at the player's home base, where the TOI pieces are placed on the of the squares marked with the same letters.

Rules

  • Players take turns making moves. 
    • all pieces move only diagonally forwards to the next empty square
    • within their home base, each piece can move in any direction
  • A trench runs across the middle of the board. To cross it, each player needs one or more bridges
    • A bridge is formed when one friendly piece moves diagonally into an unoccupied square in the trench. The TOI pieces cannot be used as bridges
  • Jumping Over Bridges: During the game, pieces can jump over any number of friendly bridges onto empty squares or directly into the opponent's home base
    • if several of your own pieces are lined up towards one of your bridges, they can all jump simultaneously if there are enough free spaces beyond the trench
    • once a piece has crossed a bridge, it cannot move back into the trench! 
    • if a player no longer has any of their own pieces on their side of the trench, their bridges can be removed and moved
  • Jumping Over Pieces: except for the bridge pieces, no other playing pieces can be jumped over
  • Wins the player that positions his pieces on the opponent's home base, just like they started in their own home base
    • The winner score comes from how many pieces the opponent was unable to bring home: one point for regular pieces, two points of 'T' and 'I', and three points for the 'O' piece
    • The Toi-pieces that are already in the opponent's home base, but not on their designated squares, are valued as if they were outside the house

 Some advices from the rulebook:  

  • Position your playing pieces so that as many as possible can cross over one of your bridges simultaneously. Bring them into the opponent's house in such a way that you don't need to move them anymore. This will save you time!
  • Make things as difficult as possible for your opponent so that they lose time. Prevent them from leaving their house and occupy their transition points. However, the disadvantages you incur in doing so should not be greater than those of your opponent.
  • Bring in the three high-value Toi-pieces in good time. They have a specific place in the house. They are worthless elsewhere!
