Scalino

Date: 2028-11-03 20:37 | Tags: old games, tile games

Scalino is a 1970 game by Peter Pallat, published at Bütehorn Spiele (among others).

The game is played on this board:

 and each player gets half of these sets of tiles (each has two different colors):


the game is from two up to four players

The goal is to build a pyramid of height four having the board as its base, with a total of forty steps, like this example:


note that there is no single top piece to place on top
of the cross' central square at level four
(so this center is not a step and, thus, is not scored)

Definition:

  • A court is any NxN area of empty positions, or placed tiles, that is surrounded by three sides

Rules:

  • On the first turn, the first player drops a friendly tile on the center of the board
  • Afterwards, on her turn, the player drops one friendly tile
    • the tile must be adjacent to another tile, of a different color, already in place
    • the tile cannot be adjacent, nor can be placed on top, to a tile of the same color
    • the tile placement cannot produce a court
    • tiles can only be placed on the next level, when the current level is completely occupied
  • The player that places the last tile to complete a level, plays again
  • If a player does not have a valid move, she must pass
  • When both players pass, the game ends and wins the player with the highest score
    •  every flight of stairs of the same color (steps can go up or down) scores as follows:
      • one step 1 point, two steps 3 points, three steps 6 points, four steps 10 points, and so on following the classic sequence of triangular numbers
    •  if there are tiles left on the reserve (meaning that the pyramid is incomplete), each square unit of these tiles counts 1 negative point
The rules offer this scoring example:
  • There's a flight of stairs of size 6 (so, 21 points). Step 'a' is not counted there since it is a branch of steps 1-6, but count as a single step (1 point). Green also has three more single steps (1 point each), and one double step (3 points). So the total Green score is 28 points.
  • The rules don't consider the remaining three steps c-b-c (that would score 6 points). I assume that it is not valid to count adjacent tiles, at the same level, for two different flights of stairs. That is, sequence c-b-c is not counted, because tiles a, 4, 5, and the top 1 were already used in scoring.

The court concept seems to exist to minimize situations where players are unable to complete the first levels of the pyramid. A player could quickly stalemate the adversary -- by preventing the completion of an early level -- if she estimates having less negative points.
