Crescendo

Date: 2030-06-14 08:04 | Tags: new games, stack pieces, tile games

Crescendo is a 1997 game by Claude Jonette, published at Filsfils International.

Each player has eight pieces. There are twelve tiles cross-shaped shared by the players. 

In the next rules, I consider a single piece as a stack of size one.

Rules

  • Setup: initially, the twelve crosses are distributed among the players. Each player, in turn, places a cross. 
    • Every new cross must touch at least two sides of crosses already in place. This continues until all twelve crosses are placed. 
    • Pieces are then placed on the twelve white squares.
    • Each player will have two pieces left to form two stacks of two pieces each. 

a possible game setup (in this case, for four players)

  • On his turn, the player moves a friendly stack
    • A stack, or part of it (ie, stacks can be split), moves orthogonally as many squares as the number of pieces it contains
    • A stack may pass over occupied squares
    • If a stack stops on a square with a friendly stack, they get stacked together
  • If the moving stack ends on an occupied square by an opponent’s stack, the player scatters the opponent's pieces onto empty squares
    • However, stacks are protected when they are on a square of the same color
    • Ko rule: A stack cannot move back where it was at the previous turn
  • Wins the player that builds a stack with all his pieces

A sensible tactic is to place your final stack on a square of the same color, to prevent its capture and dispersal. There is a tension between building the final stack and having stacks to attack and disperse enemy stacks. Until at least the final race starts to see who stacks all pieces first.
