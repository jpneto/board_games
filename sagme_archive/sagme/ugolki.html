Ugolki

Date: 2029-08-24 13:35 | Tags: older games, traditional games

Ugolki is, according to Wikipedia, a traditional European game from the late 18th century. However, no sources are given.

the initial setup

Each player has 16 stones placed in opposite 4x4 areas on a 8x8 board.

Rules

  • On his turn, the player moves one of his pieces
  • A piece either:
    • moves to an adjacent empty square
    • jumps over adjacent pieces, of either color, landing in the immediate next square that must be empty; jumps can be multiple
  • All moves must end in a square farther away from the player corner, given the moving piece's initial square
  • When no more moves are available, wins the player with more pieces in his opposite 4x4 corner

Why do I find it interesting to pinpoint Ugolki's origins? There is still, afaict, an ongoing mystery about an old passage that mentions Halma's relationship to a British game called Hoppity. From this excellent post about the history of Halma by Bruce Whitehill:
 
HALMA was invented between 1883 and 1884 by George Howard Monks, an American thoracic surgeon at Harvard Medical School.  Robert Monks, brother of G.H. Monks, discovered the British game of HOPPITY during a trip to England in 1883 or 1884.  He described the game to his brother George, apparently, who then took a couple of suggestions from HOPPITY and created HALMA, but not in such a way as to infringe on HOPPITY’s copyright.

But what's the mystery? There is no documentation, or surviving boards, about this elusive Hoppity!

One solution to this mystery is that George Monks just met people playing Ugolki, which has very similar ludemes to Halma. Being a traditional game there is no patent documentation, and being played with Checkers material, there are no specific gaming material for it. It would not be hard to find Russians in 19th century England. Many political exiles and Jewish people from Russia emigrated to England thanks to the continuous persecutions from the Russian Imperial regime.
