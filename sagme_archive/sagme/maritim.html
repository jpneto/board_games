Maritim

Date: 2027-02-12 09:51 | Tags: rotating pieces, old games

Maritim is a 1987 game by Reinhold Wittig, published at Blatz among others.

It is played in the following hexagonal board:

Each player has one ship (the game is for two up to six players). There is also six lighthouses and one buoy.

Rules:

  • Initially the ships start at the middle hexes of the left edge of the board (setup for two players). The second player places the six lighthouses and their light beam directions (represented by the wooden sticks). Here's one possible setup (the B is the buoy, the numbers are the boats):

  • On his turn, the player has a budget of nine points, that he uses to perform actions.
    • rotate a lighthouse by one-sixth of a circle costs one point
    • move a lighthouse to an adjacent empty circle (the light direction stays the same) costs two points
    • move the ship in a straight line; each circle costs one point; the ship can jump over the adversary ship (the jump is just a regular movement, and so costs one point), but not lighthouses or the buoy
    • the player's ship must reach, at the end of his turn, an empty circle that intersects two non-parallel light beams, otherwise he forfeits all points and is allowed to drift the ship to an adjacent empty circle in any direction
    • if there are points left, the player might use them to still move or rotate lighthouses
  • Wins the player that first reaches the starting edge, after rounding the turning buoy (either clockwise or counterclockwise)
