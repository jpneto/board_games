Quarrée

Date: 2029-11-09 15:40 | Tags: dice, new games

Quarrée is a 1991 uncredited game, published at Demand Spieleverlag.


Each player has seven dice with special markings, which are used as pieces (they are not used as random generators).

From the BGG description:

A game for two players with a very light billiards theme, where dice are used to represent the balls. Most of your dice begin showing a single pip, which means that it can move one space. Once you move a single-pip die, you must then turn the die so that it shows two pips, which means that the next time it is moved, it must now move two spaces, but only in a straight line (unless it is banked off of the edge of the board). The final three faces of the die are not numbers but symbols that give the die special movement abilities, such as the ability to jump over another die. Once a die has been moved six time, it reverts back to a single-pip die. The game ends when one player has managed to move a die into an opposing pocket.

Quarrée

M. Merlin (translation: Luc Forest)
2 players - 15 to 30 min

Materials:

  • 1 game board / billiard table 
  • 9 dice per player (each die has the 6 symbols shown here)

Game Setup:

  • On each side, the dice are positioned as shown in the figure opposite.

Object of the Game:

  • You must place one of your dice in one of the two holes in the opponent's camp.  
Gameplay:

Each player plays one of their dice in turn until the end of the game.  

Moving the Dice: A die is moved from a square in the direction of one of its longest sides, i.e., not diagonally. The distance and type of movement of a die depend on the symbol on its top face:  

[Table describing die movement based on symbol]

"These dice can be moved up to a distance of 1, 2, or 3 squares in a straight line."  

"The square can be moved up to a distance of 4 squares in a straight line and can jump over a square occupied by another die (friend or enemy). The circle can be moved up to a distance of 4 squares, changing direction as desired. The tail can be moved up to a distance of 4 squares in a straight line. If the movement ends on a square already occupied by another of your own dice, that die is pushed and, in turn, moved up to four squares in a straight line in one of the directions from which the pushing die cannot come. The face of the die is not taken into account for this movement and is not changed."
