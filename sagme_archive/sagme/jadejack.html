JadeJack

Date: 2030-02-01 09:38 | Tags: new games, stack pieces

JadeJack, aka Hi-Jack, is a 2002 game by Barrie Evans.

The game is played on an initially empty square board. Suggested sizes are 8x8 and 11x11.

Definitions:

  • A stack consists of one or more pieces of either color
  • A stack claims a set of squares depending on its size

a stack of size 4 adds an extra orthogonal step plus another ring, and so on...

  • Blocking: a stack has no claim on a square, in an orthogonal direction, if all intervening squares are occupied

  • A square is controlled by a player if he has more claims to it than the adversary

Rules

  • On his turn, the player either:
    • passes
    • drops a piece on an empty square, if the player's claims are at least equal to the adversary claims
    • drops a piece over a friendly stack 
    • drops a piece over an enemy stack, if the player's claims are at least equal to the adversary claim plus the height of the enemy stack
  • When both players consecutively pass, the game ends, winning the player that controls more empty squares
    • optionally, the players can also score how many friendly stacks including enemy pieces (these are called hi-jacked stacks)

More information on the game's website. The game was presented in Abstract Games #14.
