Antipalos, Bridg-It, September

Date: 2027-04-16 08:31 | Tags: old games, connection

Antipalos is a 1982 game from Martin Collier and Roland L. Tesh, published by Antipalos Games.

The board is a grid of 17x17 intersections:

The players have 26 pieces each. The board starts empty.

Rules:

  • The player, on her turn, drops a friendly piece between two diagonally adjacent points; that diagonal must be free of other pieces 
    • when all the pieces are already on board, the players must pick a friendly piece on board and place it on another diagonal
  • Wins the player that makes a path of friendly stones connecting her two opposite edges (one player has North/South, the other has East/West)

Notice that it is possible from enemy paths to cross each other over an intersection, e.g., the next position is legal:

Also notice that in the board coexist two different sub-boards: two parallel pieces next to the other are on 'different worlds', or 'phases' (e.g., the two parallel blue pieces in the previous diagram). Paths on different phases cannot join together. This is an important tactical consideration.

Antipalos is one of several 'pure' connection games that were designed in the second half of the 20th century. The best known examples are Hex (the prototypical connection game), Y, and Twixt (another great classic). Other similar game is 1960's Bridg-It,

Another game that follows Bridg-It 'two-boards-in-one' approach is 1985's September by  Danny Kishon.

The difference here is that players drop polyomino-walls, instead of single domino-walls. Each player has seven dominoes, three corners, one T, one U, one + and one 3x1 piece. When all of his pieces are on the board, the player must pick one corner or straight piece and reposition it.
