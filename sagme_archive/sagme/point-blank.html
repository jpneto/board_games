Point Blank

Date: 2027-07-16 12:52 | Tags: rotating pieces, old games

Point Blank is a 1979 uncredited game, published by Clipper (among others).

The game is played on a 6x6 board,

and each player has 18 octogonal pieces labeled with arrows to give them a direction.

Rules:

  • Initially, each player drops a friendly piece on an empty position, choosing one of its eight possible directions
  • On his turn, the player must drop a new piece in the immediate position pointed by his previous dropped piece 
    • The arrow of the new piece must point to a position that must be empty in the next turn
    • Consider the board as a torus, i.e, rows and columns circle back horizontally and vertically (but not diagonally)
  • Wins the player that stalemates the adversary

The original rules:

An obvious tactical approach is to split the board into two areas, trying to reserve the biggest area, in order to have enough space to move. The fact that the board is a torus makes this (fortunately) more difficult to achieve.

In 1995, David Smith, proposed Survival, a similar game played on a hexagonal board with edges of length three (so, with 19 hexes).

Each player has 10 identical pieces of their color marked with an arrow that indicates where that player's next piece must go.

1. Ohs plays first by placing one own piece on any space with its arrow    pointing in any direction.
2. Eks does the same by placing any own piece on any remaining vacant space.
3. On every subsequent move, each player must place one own piece in turn on any remaining vacant space in the direction the arrow on his or her previously played piece is pointing.
4. The first player who cannot continue loses the game.
