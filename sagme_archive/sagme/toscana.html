Toscana

Date: 2028-07-14 07:20 | Tags: new games, tile games

Toscana is a 2001 game by Niek Neuwahl, published at Piatnik.

The game is played on a 17x17 board. Each player has 16 tiles of size 2x4, each tile with six squares of the player's color and two squares of the opponent's color. The red squares are roofs of the city of Toscana, while the white squares are plazas.

The game also includes some extra pieces that are used in counting procedures, but are not essential during the match.

Rules:

  • Players shuffle their tiles and place them in a stack; this stack defines the placing order
  • The first tile of the first player must cover the center of the board
  • On her turn, the player drops her next tile (taken from her stack of tiles) in an empty area
    • at least one square must be adjacent to a square of the same color from a previous tile (of either player)
    • the adversary has one opportunity to veto the player's placement; when the adversary exercises this veto, that tile goes to the bottom of the stack, and the player drops the next tile
  • Wins the player that has more tiles in her biggest connected group of roofs/plazas

don't forget: we count tiles, not unit squares

The rules say that players should play two matches, and the winner is the one with the highest summed score. This is to counter the first player's advantage. Another way to balance the game would be to use a regular Pie rule: one player drops a tile anywhere on the board, the other player decides who starts.

Strategy tips from Russ:

The first player seems to have a slight advantage. Basically your goal is to spread yourself as widely as you can, while hindering your opponent's efforts to do the same. When you get a tile that has two separated zones of your color, they are sometimes not so useful for expanding yourself, as they often cause a second zone in your color to start forming, which can sometimes prove difficult to connect to the first, especially if your opponent plays to hinder such a connection. So when you get a 2-zone tile, it is often better to use it against your opponent's growth.

Since victory is based on who has the largest connected zone, making your opponent small is often just as good as making yourself big. Remember to consider all possible places for your tile, with an eye to maximizing the difference between your score and your opponents, not just obsessing about how to make your own score as big as possible. Sometimes it's definitely better to use the tile hurt the opponent's zone a lot than to help your own zone somewhat.

If you can keep a single big zone growing, while managing to split your opponent into 2 or more major zones, then you will probably win.

There are lots of little tactical geometry decisions to make. Because the 2x4 tiles do not have to be played in neat alignment (and indeed the board is 17x17), there will be various little 1-square wide "alleys" between tiles. Creating these to consume an opponent's areas with a smaller number of tiles is good. You want to pack as many connected tiles as efficiently as possible into your own areas, while forcing your opponent's areas to have lots of unplayable wasted space and therefore fewer tiles.
