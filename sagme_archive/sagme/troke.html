Troke

Date: 2029-09-07 11:59 | Tags: old games

Troke is a 1956 game by James Brunot and Arpad Rosti, published at Schowanek and Production & Marketing Company (among others).


Each player has 12 single-pieces: four towers, four walls and four moats, that fit together to make a castle:

There are, thus, four possible multi-piece combinations: tower+wall, tower+moat, wall+moat, and the entire castle.

For two players (the game also works with three and four participants) both place their pieces as four castles in their first row; the two players facing each other.

Rules

  • On his turn, the player moves a friendly single-piece
    • A piece can move one step forward (including diagonally) and sideways, but not backwards
    • The movement can be to an empty circle, or to one occupied by a single or multiple-piece that does not have duplicate parts with the moving piece (of either color)
    • If the moving piece joined other pieces, then it must move one extra step
      • A joined piece can transport enemy pieces backwards
      • However, enemy pieces cannot return to their home row 
      • If the player cannot perform this extra step, then the initial join is invalid
  • Wins the player that moves all his 12 pieces to the opposite row

The rules mention a scoring system based on joining with enemy pieces (the rules confusingly call it capturing), but it is a bit convoluted and which, anyway, seems superfluous (check the official rules below).

Here's a quote from Bruce Whitehill about the game:

The movement and goals are the same [as Halma/Chinese Checkers], except that each playing piece, a “castle,” is divided into three sections: the moat, the wall, and the tower.  Each player has four castles, and each segment moves independent of the others unless captured; a capture can only be made by a different type of piece or by two different pieces.  A player can “capture” his own pieces.  When captured, the capturing piece gets to move the entire unit one space for each piece captured.  For example, if a wall captures a moat, the wall and moat will move one additional space together; if a wall captures a moat that is with a tower, the entire unit (the wall, the moat, and the tower) moves two spaces.  It is also possible to capture segments one at a time, so that, for example, the wall can first capture a moat, and then move as a unit and capture a tower, allowing another move for the entire unit.  As a castle (three segments together–which can belong to three different players), the pieces are not subject to attack; but in order to move, one of the segments must leave the secure castle.  Also known as CASTLE CHECKERS, the game was produced in 1956 by  Selchow & Richter (Production & Marketing Co.), the makers of SCRABBLE.

Troke is a very interesting Halma-like game.
