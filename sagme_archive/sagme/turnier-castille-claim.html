Turnier, Castille, Claim

Date: 2026-07-21 14:19 | Tags: old games

Turnier is a 1976 game by Alex Randolph, published at Parker Brothers.

The game consists of eight board sections, each with 12 positions, some rectangular, some L-shaped.

Each player has 18 pieces (towers) and three flag pieces that can be attached.

Rules 

  • In the setup, players alternatively drop towers on empty positions. Then, players remove four enemy towers (also in alternate turns).
  • On their turn, a player may move one of their pieces one space orthogonally to an adjacent empty position.
    • A piece may jump over an adjacent piece in a straight line if the space beyond is free.
    • Multiple jumps are allowed, and after each jump, the direction may change.
  • There are several ways to capture an opponent’s piece:
    • If, by moving one’s own piece, an opponent’s piece becomes trapped between two pieces of one or two players, it is removed.
    • A piece is also removed if it stands at the edge of the board and an opposing piece moves onto the adjacent space directly across from it on the opposite edge.
    • After a capture, the player immediately takes another turn if the same piece can make another capture. If a move allows multiple captures, all those pieces are removed.
  • When only pieces of one color remain on a game section, the owner receives a flag, which is placed on any one of their pieces in that area. This board is now conquered, even if later enemy pieces can enter it. 
    • The conquest can only be undone by capturing the flagged piece.
    • When it’s their turn, a player may transfer their flag to another piece of the same color within that section.
    • If a flagged piece leaves the area, the flag is immediately removed.
    • Removed flags cannot be reused later.
  • The game ends when one player has three flags on board.

The game has one big problem -- probably what was called a 'fatal flaw' by the author --, after one flag is captured, that player cannot win the game. If the other player also has a captured flag, the game is automatically a draw, since it becomes unwinnable.

A variant was proposed where flags are not used, and one player scores each conquest immediately. This modification does not suffer the same problem.

In 1983, the author made a new iteration of this idea: Claim (later called Die Wikinger Kommen).

It seems that in these new versions, there are slightly different rulings regarding reclaiming board sections:

  • in 1983: the enemy flag is removed and replaced with one of yours on any vacant dot of that section; the flag is returned to its owner
  • in  1994: the conquered flag is given back to the owner and replaced on the same position with an own one
  • in 1996 a Ko rule was added: a player is not allowed to do the same move within three turns without either trespassing a territory or threatening an opponents piece
