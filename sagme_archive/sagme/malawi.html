Malawi

Date: 2028-03-17 17:54 | Tags: old games, stack pieces

Malawi is a 1986 game by Gerhard E. Kodys, published at Piatnik.

The game is played on a 6x6 board with the following setup:

Each player has six stack-like pieces where it's possible to insert rings (to a maximum of six rings). Initially, each piece starts with two rings.

Rules:

  • On her turn, a player can either:
    • slide a piece a number of empty squares exactly equal to the number of its rings
      • the movement is orthogonal or diagonal in a straight line (no jumps or changes of direction)
    • share the rings of a piece among the other friendly pieces
      • the player removes all the rings and places, at most, one ring per friendly piece
      • if the piece has six rings, the final ring is removed from play (there are only five pieces to share the rings)
    • capture an enemy piece in moving range
      • capturing means removing the rings of the attacked piece (the stack itself is never removed), the rings are no longer used
      • both the attacker and the attacked don't move 
      • the initial numbers of rings of both pieces are irrelevant (there's no notion of strength proporcional to the rings a piece has)
  • Wins the player that moves a friendly piece to her last row, or stalemates the adversary (e.g., a player without rings can no longer move)

The players must learn to distribute their rings to create robust positions that can attack at several places, making it hard for the opponent to properly defend. Large stacks have more moving range, but less maneuverability and have a higher cost of being captured (and players only have a budget of 12 rings...). Since pieces must move exactly the number of rings they have, it's possible to block attacks. 

Overall, Malawi has a quite interesting and original concept.
