Sqez

Date: 2028-01-07 13:25 | Tags: old games, pencil & paper

Sqez is a 1973 game by Donald Laycock, published at Games and Puzzles magazine.

The board consists of a rectangular grid of points of any size (it is suggested that the grid such be larger than 8x8).

Rules:

  • On his turn, the player draws a rectangle by connecting the dots of its perimeter.
    • The rectangle must not pass over a previously line, but lines can cross and two rectangles can share corners.
  • Wins the player that moves last
    • optional rule: if a player starts drawing an invalid rectangle, he loses

Eàmon Bloomfield quotes Donald Laycock (the author) about the game:

Some games players have no sooner mastered a basic game than they want variants, and today the games inventor must cater for them by devising 'open-ended' games. So here are some possible variations for Sqez, none of them really tested:

  • Additional shapes can be permitted — triangles, trapezoids, octagons; or else, any quadrilateral, and, ultimately, any shape at all!
  • Players can be restricted in their choice of shapes — that is, one player may place only oblongs, another only triangles, another only octagons — and so on.

It need hardly be pointed out, however, that the strategy for any of these variants will differ radically from that of the basic game.

Why the name Sqez? Well, firstly, it suggests 'squares', and, secondly, there is a lot to be said for any new word added to the language that contains a q without a following u. But the word was in fact invented at Newcastle University College (Newcastle, New South Wales, Australia, that is) in about 1955, to designate a mythical deity in an undergraduate send-up cult. I have resurrected the name for a more serious purpose.
