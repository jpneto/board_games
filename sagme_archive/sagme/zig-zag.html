Zig Zag

Date: 2028-10-06 07:32 | Tags: old games, tile games

Zig Zag is a 1977 game by Theo Coster and Ora Coster, published at Milton Bradley.

The game is played in the following board:

The picture also shows the game pieces, a set of thirteen polyhexes per player: five single hexes, four double hexes, three triple, and one quadruple. The board, initially empty, consists of ten different zones, where the pieces fit in groups.

Rules:

  • On her turn, the player drops one friendly piece over empty positions at one available board zone
    • When a player drops the last piece that fills a zone, she scores that zone's value (one point per unit hex, so a zone with area N values N points), and moves again
    • If a player does not have a valid move, she passes her turn
  • When both players pass, the game ends winning the player with the highest score

Notice that the first player can start with 7 points by filling the zones with area three and four. The rules state that this is a bad move, since it will prevent gaining larger scores, but it is indeed a great start. And I'm not sure if the first player is not able, afterwards, to sabotage the scoring on the larger zones (by placing a piece in the middle of the available area).
