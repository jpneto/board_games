Freez!

Date: 2027-11-05 13:35 | Tags: dice, new games

Freez! is a 1993 game by Judith Lahore, published at Freez! Games.

The game is played on a 8x8 board,

Each player has 10 dice, but the game only uses faces 1, 2, 3.

Rules:

  • Pieces can be alive of frozen.
  • On her turn, the player moves one friendly live piece
    • A piece moves exactly the number of steps given by its top face
      • pieces move orthogonally or diagonally in a straight line, but can optionally change direction every time they pass thru a starred square
      • the direction change cannot be a right angle (90º), only oblique angles (45º or 135º) are valid, either left of right


examples of valid direction changes

  • Captures are by replacement, but only at the last square in the piece's moving range
    • the captured piece becomes frozen, and is placed on an adjacent square; the chosen square cannot block an enemy threat against the attacker (unless there's no other option, or the attacker has more than one threat to deal with)
  • A capture ends the player's turn
  • If the movement didn't result in a capture, the player, optionally, can also:
    • change the die's top face (from 3 to 2, from 2 to 1, from 1 to 3)
    • move one friendly frozen die to an adjacent empty square
  • Wins the player that completes a wall of frozen dice (mixed colors are allowed) that separates both armies

Given that there are only 20 dice, and the minimal length wall is eight, it is necessary to produce lots of frozen pieces. However, enemy frozen pieces are good for the opponent, so why freeze them? The game's goal does not seem to make a lot of sense. If captures were mandatory the game would gain a misère theme, and the building of a frozen army would provide, possibly, an interesting dynamic (White walkers, anyone?).
