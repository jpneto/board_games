Suffragetto

Date: 2030-02-08 20:54 | Tags: older games

Suffragetto is a 1909 uncredited game, published by Women's Social and Political Union (WSPU) and Sargeant Bros Ltd.

The game is played in the following board:


initial setup

The 2x3 areas are the player's buildings.

Each player has 21 pieces, five large (the leaders) and sixteen small (the suffragettes/the police).

Rules

  • On her turn, the player either:
    • moves a friendly piece to an (orthogonal or diagonal) adjacent empty square (except to the areas marked Prison, Prison Yard, Hospital, Hospital Grounds, or to their own buildings)
    • jumps over an adjacent piece (of either type or color) landing in the immediate next square that must be empty
      • jumps can be multiple
      • suffragettes can disable (or the police can arrest) when jumping diagonally over an enemy piece
      • a leader can disable/arrest when jumping (orthogonally or diagonally) over an enemy piece 
      • a disabled piece is placed on the Hospital area, while an arrested piece is placed on the Prison area
      • pieces on the arena (pink squares) can be arrested (if it is a suffragette) or disabled (if it is a police) 
      • pieces on the streets (yellow squares) cannot be arrested/disabled (but they can still be jumped over)
    • if both the white areas contain twelve or more pieces each, players can swap up to six pieces of equal type (a leader exchanges with a leader, a regular piece with a regular piece)
      • these exchanged pieces are placed in the respective green areas, where they can start moving
      • while the green areas are occupied, no more exchanges can occur
  • Wins the player that moves six friendly pieces to the adversary's building

This is a historically interesting game. It was published as a means to gather funds for the WSPU movement, but also as a political statement, a way to share with the public the plight and pressures they suffered defending Women's rights. A nice example that not all abstract games need to be themeless.
