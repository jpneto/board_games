Gol-skuish and Pretwa

Date: 2026-11-27 22:28 | Tags: traditional games

Gol-skuish is a traditional game from India. 

The board is made of seven concentric circles divided in six sectors:

the initial setup

Each player has 21 pieces that are placed initially as shown.

Rules:

  • On his turn, a player either:
    • moves a friendly piece to an adjacent empty position (pieces follow the board lines)
    • captures an enemy piece by jumping over it, and landing on the immediate next position that must be empty; captures are mandatory and multiple
  • Wins the player that captures all enemy pieces

The game was reported in [1],

The game is quite drawish. Some options proposed to improve its decidability:

  • The first player reduced to five pieces loses the game
  • A player wins if it stalemates the adversary
  • If a position is reached where no captures are being made, wins the player with more pieces

This page proposes a different winning criteria: 

Since draws may occur frequently between two equally skillful players, we suggest the following "tiebreak" rule (even though it is not historically authentic): If there are only two or three pieces left on the board (i.e., one against one, or one against two), then the player who is first able to occupy the center point wins the game. (Thus, in a two-against-two situation, each player should look for a way to sacrifice a piece at a time when his other piece can occupy the center.)
