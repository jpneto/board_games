Bizingo

Date: 2028-02-04 17:12 | Tags: older games, checkers, new games

Bizingo is, apparently, a c.1850 uncredited American game, published at Fratelli Fabbri Editori (among others).

The game is played in the following board:

the game's (asymmetrical) setup

Each player has 16 regular pieces (the men) and two taller pieces (the captains).

Rules:

  • On his turn, the player moves one of his friendly pieces
    • Pieces move any corner adjacent empty position of the same color
    • If one or more men get surrounded by all sides, they get captured
    • To capture the Captain, the method is the same, but one of the attackers must be a Captain
    • At the edges (two liberties only), a surrounded piece is only captured if at least one of the attackers is a Captain
    • If a piece moves into a surrounded position, it gets captured unless the move results in an enemy capture
  • Wins the player that reduces the opponent's army to less than three pieces

The source available for this game is 1979 R.C. Bell's The Boardgame Book. All later references spring from this one:

There is no reference of Bizingo on 1952 Murray's book, neither on 1939 Depew's The Cokesbury Game Book, and also nothing in Malloy's American Games (a collector's guide), or Dennis' Antique American Games. Bell does not include it in his more famous work, Board and Table Games from many Civilizations. Its 2nd edition is from 1969, so Bell could have known about Bizingo during the 1970s.
