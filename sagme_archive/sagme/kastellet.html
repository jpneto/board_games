Kastellet

Date: 2029-09-28 07:53 | Tags: older games

Kastellet is an 1892 game by Henry William Croome Smith.

The game is played on a hexagonal board with the following setup:


Each player has five flags (placed on the first row), four maces (at the sides of the 3rd and 4th row), and three lances (in the middle hexes of the 3rd row). The players also have an extra neutral red piece called the bar (that starts off-board).

The red central hex is the Point. It is said that a player covers the Point, if at least one of her pieces could be moved to the Point.

Rules:

  • On her turn, the player moves one of her pieces
    • Flags move forward to one adjacent hex
      • if a flag reaches the middle hex of the player's last row, in the next turn (if it was not captured), the player may use her turn to change it for the player's own bar, which is then placed on any empty hex (and it will stay there until the end of the game)
    • Maces move to any adjacent hex
    • Lances jump to the next hex of the same color (so up to six hexes on the middle of the board, three hexes on the edge, and just two hexes if it is on a corner)
  • Captures are by replacement 
  • Pieces cannot move into the Point
  • Loses the player that, at the beginning of her turn, does not cover the Point.

This seems like a Chess game but does not have a royal piece. The author proposes an original goal of protecting the central hex, which I don't recall seeing in any other board game.

As the author says:

The central position marked R in the diagram is the point: this being a non-playing position constitutes, I believe, a 'essential difference between this board and others which may have been produced of a somewhat similar pattern.

More than 130 years after, I think he is still right.

The neutral piece can serve as a blocking unit, but it's quite hard to get it during play. I doubt its usefulness compensates for the extra complexity it brings to the ruleset.

The game was patented as #US521737.
