Jati

Date: 2027-03-26 16:17 | Tags: old games, moku

Jati is a 1965 game by Keith Havens, published by W. Nostheide Verlag.

Each player has 18 pieces, plus two booster pieces (one marked with II, another with III).

Rules:

  • On her turn, the player drops a friendly piece on an empty square, which must be orthogonally adjacent to any piece of either color (with an exception for the first player's move)
  • When all pieces are dropped, wins the player with highest score:
    • any orthogonal row of five friendly pieces scores 5 points, plus 2 points for any extra piece in that row
    • any diagonal row of four pieces scores 8 points, plus 5 points for any extra piece in that row
    • for any row that includes a booster piece II (of either player), the score is doubled
    • for any row that includes a booster piece III (of either player), the score is tripled 
    • notice for a row with 2+ boosters, its score is compounded
    • boosters do not count as pieces within the rows they belong to, but they count as connectors of disjoint lines of friendly pieces that make the row

Ralf Gering comments of BGG:

Well, the game is not bad. Double attacks occur frequently and a fundamental strategy appears to be to make diagonals 3 tiles long, which may later be extended to number at least four tiles. The very limited number, however, provided in the original gameset (18 normal tiles + 2 multipliers) makes it rather unlikely that a player will ever be forced to do a move, that enables his opponent to make a diagonal of four tiles or even more. A better gameplay results if Jati is played with an infinite number of ordinary tiles (still just two multipliers for each player). Then the endgame reminds of Dots & Boxes and Score Four. Nonetheless, I would still prefer Score Four or Dots & Boxes, which are deeper games.
