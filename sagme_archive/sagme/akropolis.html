Akropolis

Date: 2028-05-26 20:01 | Tags: old games, stack pieces

Akropolis is a 1981 game by Walter Wolf Windisch, published at Bütehorn Spiele.

The game is played on a 12x12 board,

This is a building game, there are three types of stackable pieces: 30 bases/ceilings, 40 columns, and 10 friezes:

Each side of each piece is marked differently, to identify which player placed it.

Definition:

  • A complete column consists of a stack of one base, two or three columns, and one ceiling (which is the same as a base)

Rules:

  • A player, on her turn, either:
    • places a base on an empty square
      • this is only valid if there are less than three empty bases (of either color), and the player has one or zero bases on the board
    • places a column on one of her stacks
      • if the player does not continue to build on this first or second column on her next turn, the adversary can also place there one of his columns
      • if the player places a third column, she must use her next turn to place a ceiling there 
    • places a frieze between two columns of the same player, with same height, and separated by one square which either is empty or has a column of smaller height
  •  Wins the player that achieve a winning pattern
    • The game proposes three possible patterns, that the players agree before the game begins: 
      • three complete columns of the same height with two adjacent friezes on a L shape
      • four complete columns of the same height with three adjacent friezes
      • four complete columns of the same height with four adjacent friezes making a square shape
    • If the winning shape does happen before all pieces are used, wins the player with highest score
      • a complete column get 1 point per column
      • a frieze is worth one of the columns where it stands

note: the rules for building a complete column implies that 

  • consecutive columns can be of different players, 
  • the base and the first column, just like the last column and the ceiling, must be of the same player

The ruleset seems to need a very good balance between player interaction and ability to build complete columns. As it is, the column placement rule promotes a building race (where the first player has clear advantage). If we relax that rule to allow players to build on each other columns, we risk cold positions where no one wants to place the second column (since the player will take it next turn). I'm not sure the game solves this adequately.


players must have steady hands on this game!
