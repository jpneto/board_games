The Two Pipers

Date: 2029-12-14 08:37 | Tags: new games

The Two Pipers is a 1993 game by Michael Petty.

The game is played in a 8x8 board with the following setup:


Each player has one piper (at d1 and e8) and eight pieces with different markings on each face: on one side they are attackers, on the other side they are sleepers. They all start as attackers.

Pipers start at the only squares that are land, and the surrounding squares (marked as [a]) are shallow water areas, the remaining squares are swaps (there's a story associated with this game).

The rules are the following, written by Ken Tidwell:

  • Movement of the Pieces
    • ATTACKERS
      • When not jumping, ATTACKERS move one space at a time diagonally to unoccupied squares.
      • An ATTACKER can jump over another creature if the space he'd land in is unoccupied. Any ATTACKER that jumps immediately falls asleep (Turn the piece over). No multiple jumps are allowed. The effects of jumps are listed below.
      • If an ATTACKER can (without violating any other rules) jump an opponent's SLEEPER, then he must do so. If there's more than one jump to choose from, the choice belongs to the owner of the ATTACKER. No other jumps are mandatory!
    • SLEEPERS
      • SLEEPERS move one space at a time horizontally or vertically to unoccupied squares.
      • A SLEEPER carrying the opponent's PIPER can't be moved.
      • If a player's PIPER is on one of his or her own SLEEPERS, then the SLEEPER (along with the PIPER) may be moved just like any other SLEEPER.
    • PIPERS
      • PIPERS may only move onto (or over) the land squares or squares occupied by SLEEPERS of either color. They may not move over or onto a SLEEPER carrying the opponent's PIPER.
      • PIPERS may move any number of squares in one direction, horizontally, vertically or diagonally, as long as rule 1 is not violated. They can't walk on the water!
      • The PIPERS may not be in adjacent squares once they are both off the land spaces.
      • If a player's PIPER is in danger of being jumped that player MUST move the PIPER to safety if possible. This takes precedence over any other rule.
  • Effects of Jumping
    • Any creature jumped by an ATTACKER of the same color is unaffected.
    • An ATTACKER jumped by an opponent's ATTACKER falls asleep.
    • A SLEEPER jumped by an opponent's ATTACKER is removed from play.
    • A PIPER is also eliminated from play if the SLEEPER he is standing on is removed. Therefore,
      • Player A cannot jump his own PIPER if he's standing on one of player B's SLEEPERS (Unless he wants to end the game by losing!).
      • Player A can eliminate player B's PIPER if he jumps the PIPER while he's standing on one of player B's SLEEPERS.
      • These are not exceptions or additions, just significant consequences of the above movement rules.
  • General Rules
    • Only the PIPERS may occupy land spaces.
    • No player may have more than two of his or her creatures (awake or asleep) in the shallow water area adjacent to a piece of land at the same time.
  • Game Play
    • Flip a coin or find a similar method to determine who goes first.
    • On a turn, a player may do ONE of the following actions:
      • Move one of his or her pieces according to the above rules OR
      • Change one of his or her creatures by flipping it over. A player may not, however, awaken a SLEEPER carrying a PIPER.
    • Alternate turns until a winning condition is met.
  • Winning the Game
    • If a player manages to get his or her PIPER across the swamp to the land where the opponent started, that player wins the game.
    • A player also wins if he or she eliminates the PIPER belonging to the opponent.
    • For purposes of winning, it is assumed that both PIPERS could stand on one of the land squares at the same time. In other words, a player can't prevent a win by planting his PIPER on the land and refusing to move it!
