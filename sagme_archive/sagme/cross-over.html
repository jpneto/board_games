Cross Over

Date: 2027-01-26 08:23 | Tags: old games, connection

Cross Over (aka Barrièrre, La Grande Muraille), is a 1985 uncredited game, published at Schmidt International.

The game is played on a 12x12 board, where players place walls between squares,

The board is initially empty and each player starts with 24 walls off-board.

Rules

  • On her turn, the player drops a friendly wall over two empty edges 
    • A wall cannot be placed between a pair of adjacent enemy walls already on board
  • The player that connects two opposite board edges (either north-south or east-west) with her walls wins the game

This is an early example of a connection game using an orthogonal board. The problem that these games need to solve are mutual deadlocks, where both players cut the other. Something like:

 

As far as I can tell, Cross Over does not give any solution to this problem, meaning there's nothing to do if players deadlock each other. So, the game carries a fatal flaw. 

There has been many different solution proposed by designers. The first game to successfully approached this problem was 1962's Twixt. At the end of the century 1999's Trellis, 2000's Gonnect and Quax proposed new solutions. Since then, many other solutions were found. If Cross Over had a solution, it would have been a missing link between Twixt, and the almost 40 years it took designers to review the deadlock problem.

There is another design problem that happens when the connection to an edge only needs a single square. In these situations players are unable to place there, since walls need two squares to be placed. Twixt also solved this by having an extra outer ring.
