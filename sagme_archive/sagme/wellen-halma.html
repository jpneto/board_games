Wellen-Halma

Date: 2028-12-01 14:09 | Tags: new games

Wellen-Halma is a 1990 game by Friedrich Schedel, published at Spielbox magazine.

The game is played in the following 8x8 board:


initial setup (red stones are neutral)

Each player has 12 pieces, and there are four neutral pieces.

Definition:

  • A pentagon is a group of five connected pieces (of the same color, or neutrals) shaped as a plus sign (+) or as a times sign (×)

Rules:

  • On her turn, the player moves one friendly piece
    • A piece moves like a Chess King, but it can do one or two steps
    • After the piece stops, each non-friendly adjacent piece is moved away one square, unless that square is occupied (these are denoted as waves, wellen in German)
      • neutral pieces cannot be moved to the outer edge (the green squares)
      • four enemy pieces, forming an orthogonal cross without center, are also immune to waves
  • Ko rule: repeating the position of the last turn is illegal
  • Wins the player that after each move (eventual wave included) either:
    • occupies a blue 2x2 area with his pieces and/or neutral pieces
    • makes two friendly pentagons
    • if both players achieve a winning pattern, wins the player that just moved

Here's the translation from German (if the reader detects a major error, please let me know):

Materials:

  • Besides the game board in chessboard size, you'll need twelve game pieces per player in one color each (e.g., black and white) and four neutral pieces in a third color (e.g., red).

Setup:

  • Like in chess, it's decided by lot who plays with White and who plays with Black. All pieces are placed on the board as shown in Figure 1. White begins.

Goal of the Game:

  • The first player to occupy one of the five blue squares with their own and/or the neutral pieces or to form a "winning cross" wins the game. A "winning cross" consists of 5 pieces on adjacent squares and looks like the number 5 on a normal die: [depiction of the '5' shape].

Gameplay:

  • The player whose turn it is moves one of their own pieces up to two spaces in any direction to a free square. Movement can be in a straight line (vertically, horizontally, or diagonally) or in a knight's move (as in chess). An own, opponent's, or neutral piece can be jumped over. Figure 3 shows some possible moves.

Impact Waves:

  • The "impact" in the target square triggers an "impact wave" that moves all opposing and neutral pieces one square away from the impact square if they are not secured.

Securing Pieces:

  • Pieces are secured if there is any piece directly behind them in the direction of movement, meaning the escape square is already occupied. Furthermore, the neutral (red) pieces cannot be moved onto the green edge. Pieces from "four-blocks" are also secured. Four-blocks are four pieces of the same color that form a Greek cross without the center square. A four-block can only be broken if the piece that triggers the impact wave itself completes it. Neutral stones that have been moved by impact waves do not trigger further waves themselves.

Figure 3 shows which impact waves a move triggers:

  • In Figure 3a, only two white pieces are moved by the black piece. The remaining four form a Greek cross and are therefore secured. The situation is similar in Figure 3b. Actually, the neutral (red) piece would also have to be moved here, but it is secured because it must not be driven onto a green edge field. In Figure 3c, the white player reaches a winning cross with his move and thus wins the game. The impact wave that the white piece triggers also helps the black player to a winning formation (occupying one of the five blue squares). However, since White is on the move and even forms a winning formation itself, the black player can no longer win.

Repetition of Moves:

  • ...are not allowed. If, after a player's move, the same position is reached on the board as after his previous move, there is a repetition of moves and the move is not allowed.

Game End:

  • The game ends when one player succeeds in forming a winning cross or completely occupying one of the five blue squares after completing their move – that is, after all impact wave displacements. Neutral pieces can be counted as your own. He is then the winner. If a winning condition is achieved for both players at the same time, the player who was on move has won. If a player creates a winning formation for the opponent with his move, then he has lost the game.
