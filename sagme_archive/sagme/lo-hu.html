Lo-Hu

Date: 2030-10-04 00:58 | Tags: older games, checkers

Lo-Hu is an 1943 uncredited game, published at Brandt und Schirmacher.


Each player has twelve single pieces and three double pieces.

The official rules:

and its translation: 

Two players play with two opposing colors. Each player has 12 simple figures and 3 double figures. First, the 12 simple figures are placed within the bordered fields on their own side.

A player moves their figures onto the black squares and their double figures remain on the white squares outside of these fields. Each player always takes turns making one move with a figure. The figures move diagonally (forwards or backwards). Simple figures may only move one or two fields. If a player moves a figure two fields, they must continue moving in that new direction.

If a simple figure enters the opponent's bordered area, this figure becomes a double figure. The simple figure is handed over to the opponent and replaced by one of their double figures. If a player has already used all their double figures, the simple figure does not enter the opponent's area. The double figure moves like the simple figures, but one field at a time. Figures can be captured by jumping over an adjacent opponent's figure. The jump is always forwards, sideways, or backwards. Several opponent's figures can be captured in one move. Captured figures are removed from the game. 

If, due to a forced move, a simple figure enters its own bordered area again, the game is won, regardless of how many figures are still in the game. Likewise, the game is won if all three double figures of the opponent have been captured.
