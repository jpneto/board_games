Labyrinth

Date: 2027-10-08 07:01 | Tags: old games, wall pieces

Labyrinth is 2000 game by Steffen Mühlhäuser, published by Steffen Spiele.

The game is played on a 9x9 board where a new maze is built for each match,


one possible initial setup

There are 100 pins/columns and 100 walls that are shared by the players. The pins should be placed at the board intersections, and made a border wall around the perimeter.

Also each player has three small pieces (the children), one large piece (the mother) and one extra marker. The children pieces have two different sides (sleeping, awaken).

Rules:

  • Initially players place walls between pairs of pins, until a maze is made
    • the first four walls must be placed where they directly connect the perimeter at each one of the board edges.

    • the maze cannot have dead-ends, i.e., U shapes (next diagram: left examples are invalid)
    • the maze can have isolated inner chambers (next diagram: rightmost example is valid)

  • The first player rotates the board and decides which side is his own, the second player then chooses another side (note: the game works up to four players)
  • Each player places the marker close to the left-most external wall (of his edge)
  • Each player gives his children pieces to the adversary, and each player drops one at an empty square inside the maze (the other two stay in reserve, for now) showing its sleeping side

example of an initial maze state
(for four players)

  • A player, on his turn, first rotates a wall and then moves a piece.
  • Wall rotation:
    • Each wall on the game board sits between two pegs. When a wall is moved, it must maintain contact with one of the two pegs, like a door to its hinge.  A wall must therefore be thought of as being swung.
    • It cannot be completely lifted out of its pins and repositioned.
    • A wall must not be moved arbitrarily.
    • During the game, dead-ends (the U shapes) can be made
    • The wall marked with an 'x' can take on four different positions by swinging.

    • If a game piece sits in the swinging path of a wall, it is carried along by the movement of the wall and moved one or more spaces. 
    • If two pieces sit next to each other, they form a blockade.

      • The first wall that each player moves must open the labyrinth on the side facing them by swinging one of the seven middle walls inwards.
      • The entrance that is created in this way remains for the entire duration of the game. It must not be closed or moved.
  • Piece movement:
    • Mothers and children pieces can slide over a straight line of empty squares (with no walls) and can make one left/right turn before sliding again
example of unconstrained possible moves
for one piece
    • As soon as the mother arrives at a neighboring square of her sleeping child, the child wakes up. There must be no wall between the mother and child. The mother wakes up her child by turning the marker downwards. The child can and must now move on its own and make its way to the exit
    • The awakening of a child always means the end of the turn, and the placement of the next sleeping child (by the adversary)
    • If a player has one or more awake children in the labyrinth, they must decide which of their figures they wish to move. The children are moved in the same way as the mother. It is permitted to divide the double move between two different game figures, i.e., each figure moves once in a straight direction (2 single moves)
    • After the awakening of the second child, the third and thus last child is placed
    • The pieces cannot pass each other. If there is another piece (of either color) in the direction of movement of a game piece, the moving piece pushes the stationary piece in front of it...


    •  until the moving piece turns into a side path...
    • ...or until the pieces reach a wall positioned perpendicular to their path. In the latter case, the pushed piece is pushed in the direction of movement over the wall onto the field behind it

    • If two arbitrary figures stand one behind the other in the direction of movement of a game piece, they form a blockade that cannot be moved
    • Such a blockade is also effective if there is a wall between the two adjacent figures. This means that a game piece can only be pushed over a wall if the field behind it is unoccupied
    • Mothers can push other mothers and children, but children can only push children
    • Outside the board: If a piece is pushed over the outer wall and is therefore on one side outside the labyrinth, it can re-enter the labyrinth in the next turn through the entrance on this side. The entire edge on one side of the labyrinth thus counts as one field
      • In a two- or three-player game, it can happen that a figure is pushed out of a side that has no entrance. In this case, the player who was pushed out may open an entrance into the labyrinth by swinging one of the seven middle wall pieces inwards. This entrance may not be closed or moved during the rest of the game
      • If a sleeping child lands outside the labyrinth, the mother must wake it up by leaving the labyrinth on the corresponding side through the entrance. Afterwards, both can re-enter the labyrinth.
      • A child that is pushed out on its own side counts as having arrived home, regardless of the condition it is in when it lands there.
  • The marker:
    • Each player has the opportunity to accumulate or use action potential during a turn.
    • If a player chooses not to rotate a wall at the start of their turn, they may move the spare stone one space to the right.
    • If a player moves a figure with only a single move (without bending/changing direction), they may also move the spare stone one space to the right.
    • It is not allowed to forgo the entire turn. Every player whose turn it is must make at least one single move with a figure.
    • A maximum of seven action steps can be saved, then the spare stone has reached the right end of the outer wall. The maze entrance is skipped.
    • A player can "spend" any number of saved action steps again. At the start of the turn, they can move additional walls or subsequently perform additional single moves with a figure, or both. 
      • For each additional wall movement and for each additional single move, the spare stone must be moved one space to the left again.
  •  The first player to leave the labyrinth with all four figures wins the game.
    • the pieces must leave thru the player's outer entrance
    • the mother may only leave the labyrinth when the last of her three children has arrived home safely
