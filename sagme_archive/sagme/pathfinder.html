Pathfinder

Date: 2029-08-31 19:43 | Tags: old games, tile games

Pathfinder is a 1954 game by William I. Barton, published at Milton Bradley.

There are the following 164 tiles available,

The goal of the game is to connect the scoring areas to get points, and to make close-offs (dead ends). Check the ruleset picture below. The game adds a rule of hidden information by making the players draw from a set of hidden tiles, but an alternative of initially give half of the tiles to each player, might still work.

The game was patented early, in 1937, with id US2162876. I think its initial paragraphs are interesting to quote here:

This invention relates to games generally and more specifically to games played on a board with movable pieces of a definite character. With many games the complex character of the rules and the Vagueness and remoteness of the object are obstacles precluding the ready acceptance of the game by new players. The costliness and complexity of the actual physical equipment itself is a serious detriment in many of the games known to the art today. Further, much of the game spirit is lost when the mind must be loaded down with involved and complex rules and a great variety of game implements.

The object of my invention is to provide a game which is governed by a few simple rules and has an obvious and simple object to guide the play. A further object is the provision of a game having simple and inexpensive equipment. A further object is the provision of a game which will allow full expression of the game spirit untrammeled by involved and complex rules and by a confusing variety of equipment. Thus the main object of my invention is to provide a simple and inexpensive means for expressing the game spirit and the spirit of play without harassing a mind already fatigued by work.  

The publishing was made almost 20 years later.
