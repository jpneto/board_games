Quatrix

Date: 2030-05-10 07:27 | Tags: new games

Quatrix is a 1997 game by Martin Schlegel, published at W. Nostheide Verlag.

Each player has nine pieces, which are put in the colored squares at the south and east edges of the picture above.

Rules

  • On his turn, each player moves one friendly piece
    • pieces slide orthogonally over a line of empty squares, being allowed to change direction, to the left or right, as many times the player wants
      • the moving range over the current row/column is given by the number of pieces (of either color) at that row/column (including the moving piece)
      • pieces must move exactly their moving range
    • pieces may not move over the two back squares 
    • the grey square can be moved over, but no piece can stop there
    • other pieces are seen as obstacles, and cannot be jumped over
  • Wins the player that first places all his pieces on the opposite last two rows 

note: I'm not completely sure about the moving range rule.

The game was reviewed in Spielbox magazine, issue 1997 #2.

A commentary from a secondary source of the rules:

In the case you would not have to use the full length of a move it would be too easy to place all tokens on the goal places of the board. But having to use the full length of the move creates challenges. It is important to put tokens first into the last row possible. The middle of the game map is of strategic importance. One gains an advantage when the opponent is forced to move a long way around own tokens. It could be useful to place to own figures in diagonal line between the two black places as a barricade.
