King's Men

Date: 2030-07-12 07:22 | Tags: older games

King's Men is a 1937 game by Elizabeth Magie Phillips, published at Parker Bros.

The game is played on the following board:


Each player has 14 pieces that start at the marked points at the edge of the board.

The field squares is the central 7x7 area; the corridors are the four rows of seven cells adjacent to the field squares; and the lodges are the four rows of seven cells where the stones start. Each lodge has a color equal to the initial stone at the setup.

Rules:

  • At each turn, each player moves a friendly stone. A stone has different ways to move depending on what cell it is over:
    • on a Lodge/Corridor - move orthogonally to an empty cell towards the field squares, or jump over an enemy stone also towards the field squares. 
    • on a Field Square - move orthogonally to an empty cell within the Field Squares, or jump over an enemy stone also within the Field Squares.
  • A stone may jump over (orthogonally or diagonally) an enemy stone, landing on the immediate next empty cell, if after the jump the stone makes at least one 3 (or more) in-a-row of friendly stones.
    • the jumped stone is placed on one empty Lodge (of the opponent's color).
    • the stones in the middle of the friendly in-a-row(s) (there may be multiple in-a-rows), may be removed from the game (the player cannot remove the stones in the extremes).
    • after every jump or capture a player has another move.
  • A player wins if he has less than three stones.
